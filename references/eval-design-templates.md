# Eval Design Templates

Binary eval criteria templates for common skill categories. Use these as starting points when running auto-research optimization loops (see `references/auto-research-methodology.md`).

---

## How to Use This File
1. Pick the skill you want to optimize
2. Grab the relevant eval template below
3. Customize criteria to your specific use case
4. Run the auto-research loop

**Golden rule:** Every criterion must be answerable with YES or NO. No scales, no "rate 1-7," no subjective vibes.

---

## Cold Email

| # | Criterion | Check |
|---|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Does the email open with a specific observation about THIS business (not a generic hook)? | YES/NO |
| 2 | Is the email under 75 words? | YES/NO |
| 3 | Does it name a specific problem/villain (not just "struggling online")? | YES/NO |
| 4 | Is the CTA a single, low-friction action (no "let's schedule a 30-minute call")? | YES/NO |
| 5 | Does the credibility line include a specific number or result? | YES/NO |
| 6 | Would the recipient know this was written for them specifically (not a template)? | YES/NO |

## Anti-Gaming Checklist

Before finalizing any eval suite, verify:
- [ ] No criterion is so narrow that the model could parrot it back verbatim and "pass"
- [ ] Criteria test OUTCOMES not MECHANICS (e.g., "reader knows what to do" vs "contains blue button")
- [ ] At least one criterion tests for specificity/personalization
- [ ] No more than 6 criteria per skill (diminishing returns beyond this)
- [ ] Each criterion is truly binary — no judgment calls on what "partially" means
