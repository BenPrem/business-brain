# Email Series Copywriting Reference

Tactical reference for writing high-converting email sequences (welcome series,
post-purchase series, nurture flows). Read this when a series skill needs deeper guidance
on a specific technique. ESP examples below use Mailchimp/Klaviyo merge-tag conventions —
swap for whatever platform the client runs.

## 1. Subject lines

### Patterns that earn opens

**Curiosity gap** — hint at value without revealing it:
- "The one thing most [audience] miss about [topic]"
- "I almost didn't send this"
- "[FNAME], a quick thought"

**Benefit-first** — lead with what they get:
- "Your guide to [desired outcome]"
- "How to [achieve thing] in [timeframe]"
- "[N] ways to get more from [product]"

**Personal/conversational** — reads like a real person:
- "Quick question for you"
- "This made me think of you"

**Social proof** — others are already in:
- "Why [number] [people like them] love [thing]"
- "You're in good company"

### Hard rules
- Under 50 characters for mobile; aim 30-40.
- The email must deliver whatever the subject promises — never bait.
- A/B tests change ONE variable at a time.
- First-name merge tags in subjects lift opens, but ration them (1 in every 3-4 emails).
- Skip spam triggers: FREE, ACT NOW, LIMITED TIME, all-caps runs, stacked punctuation.

## 2. Preview text

The preheader is the 40-90 character snippet after the subject in most inbox views — a
second chance at the open.

### Patterns
- **Extend the subject:** subject = hook, preview = context.
- **Answer the curiosity:** subject raises a question, preview hints at the answer.
- **Add specificity:** subject is warm, preview adds one concrete detail.
- **Conversational aside:** "(Plus a little surprise inside)".

### Hard rules
- Never repeat the subject line in the preview.
- Never leave it blank — the client pulls body text and it usually looks broken.
- 40-90 characters; assume mobile cuts at ~40.
- Nothing load-bearing lives ONLY in preview text — many clients hide it.

## 3. Opening lines

The first 1-2 sentences decide whether they keep reading.

**Direct address:** "Hey [FNAME], I wanted to share something with you."
**Shared experience:** "You know that feeling when [relatable moment]?"
**Bold statement:** "Most [category] advice is wrong."
**Gratitude:** "I'm so glad you're here." (welcome email 1 only — don't repeat)
**Callback:** "Remember when I mentioned [thing from last email]?" (mid-series)
**Story opener:** "Last week, something happened that I have to tell you about."

### Never open with
- "I hope this email finds you well" — wastes the prime real estate.
- "In today's fast-paced world..." — cliché filler.
- The subject line restated verbatim.
- A long paragraph. Opener = 1-2 sentences, max.

## 4. Body copy

### The one-idea rule
One core idea per email. Two ideas = two emails. The reader should be able to summarize
the email in a single sentence.

### Rhythm
- Alternate 1-sentence paragraphs with 2-3 sentence paragraphs.
- Use a single-line sentence for emphasis.
- Like this.
- Break any paragraph past three sentences.

### The micro-story arc
1. **Hook** — the opening line grabs attention.
2. **Tension** — a problem, question, or relatable struggle.
3. **Turn** — the insight, solution, or shift.
4. **Landing** — connect back to the reader and their next step.

### Bridging to the CTA
The move from content to CTA should feel earned, never abrupt:
- "If this resonates, here's the next step..."
- "That's exactly why we built [product]..."
Never drop a CTA button without at least one transition sentence.

## 5. CTAs

### Button copy
- First person converts better: "Get My Copy" over "Get Your Copy".
- Specific beats generic: "Read the First Chapter" over "Learn More".
- Start with a verb; stay under 5 words.

### Placement
- ONE primary CTA per series email — non-negotiable.
- Place it after enough context that clicking feels natural.
- Longer emails may add a mid-body text link plus the end button.
- No above-the-fold CTAs in early nurture emails.

### Links
- Every CTA links to a specific destination, never a generic homepage.
- Soft CTAs (blog, resource) → the specific piece. Direct CTAs (buy, enroll) → the
  specific product page.
- Unknown URL at draft time → describe the destination in the internal notes.

## 6. Personalization

### Merge tags
Use the ESP's tags for first name, last name, email, and (if e-commerce is connected)
product name. Conventions differ per platform — confirm before drafting.

### Using first-name well
- Always set a fallback ("friend", "there").
- Once in the greeting is enough for most emails; never more than twice in one body.
- In subject lines: sparingly (1 in 3-4 emails).

### Dynamic content
Flag in internal notes where variants may be needed: different product purchased →
different quick-win tips; different lead magnet → different welcome content; geography →
timezone-sensitive sends.

## 7. ESP build notes

Attach these as internal notes for whoever assembles the series in the platform:

- **Template:** simple single-column layouts for series emails. Heavy design reduces
  readability and kills the personal-letter feel.
- **Images:** 0-1 per email; if one is used, specify what it should show.
- **Font:** match the brand guide; default to a clean system font at 14-16px body.
- **Mobile:** assume 60%+ of reads are mobile — short paragraphs, large tap targets.
- **Footer:** unsubscribe link, physical address, and a permission reminder ("You're
  getting this because you [signed up at / purchased from] [brand]").

### Automation setup notes (per email)
- The trigger (signup, purchase, tag application).
- Delay from the previous email, in days.
- Skip/suppress conditions (e.g. "purchases mid-series → move to post-purchase series").
- Tags to apply on engagement (opened, clicked, didn't open).

## 8. Series pacing

### Welcome series (cold leads)
| Email | Day | Purpose |
|-------|-----|---------|
| 1 | 0 (immediate) | Welcome + deliver the promised thing |
| 2 | 2-3 | Origin story / connection |
| 3 | 4-5 | Pure value, no ask |
| 4 | 7-8 | Social proof / community |
| 5 | 10-12 | Soft pitch |
| 6 | 14-15 | Gentle close |

### Post-purchase series
| Email | Day | Purpose |
|-------|-----|---------|
| 1 | 0 (immediate) | Thank you + confirmation |
| 2 | 2-3 (or on delivery) | Quick win / first-use tip |
| 3 | 5-7 | Story behind the product |
| 4 | 10-12 | Social proof + community |
| 5 | 14-16 | Cross-sell / upsell |
| 6 | 20-21 | Review ask + loyalty loop |

### Timing rules
- Never two emails on the same day.
- Gaps widen as the series progresses (2-day gaps grow to 5-day gaps).
- Schedule for 9-10 AM recipient-local time; Tues-Thurs outperform Mondays and Fridays.
- A key action mid-series (purchase, upgrade) should reroute or end the sequence.
