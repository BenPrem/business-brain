# Hormozi Frameworks — $100M Offers + $100M Leads

**Attribution first.** This skill applies frameworks Alex Hormozi published in *$100M
Offers* and *$100M Leads* (Acquisition.com book series). It summarizes the concepts at
working altitude so an agent can apply them to real acquisition problems — it does not
replace the books. **Buy the books** — they are inexpensive, and the depth (examples,
math, edge cases) lives there, not here.

Use when designing offers, building lead systems, advising a client on acquisition, or
framing a proposal's growth plan.

---

## Core definitions

- **Lead** — anyone you can contact.
- **Engaged lead** — a lead who has shown interest. Advertising exists to produce
  *engaged* leads, not raw contacts.
- **Advertising** — making your stuff known. More people knowing = more sold. Most
  acquisition problems are volume problems wearing a tactic costume.

---

## The Value Equation (from $100M Offers)

Value rises with **dream outcome** and **perceived likelihood of achievement**; it falls
with **time delay** and **effort/sacrifice**. Evaluate every offer, ad, and lead magnet
against all four levers:

- Raise: the outcome promised, and the proof it will actually happen (case studies,
  guarantees, specificity).
- Lower: how long results take, and how much work the buyer must do.

Most weak offers fail on the bottom half — they promise a fine outcome that arrives
slowly and demands effort. Fix the denominators first.

## The Grand Slam Offer

An offer so good the prospect feels foolish declining. Build it by stacking:

1. **Dream outcome** — sell the transformation, not the deliverable.
2. **Perceived likelihood** — proof, guarantee, specificity.
3. **Speed** — shrink time-to-first-result deliberately.
4. **Ease** — name what the buyer does NOT have to do.

Add a **value stack**: list each component with a stated standalone value so the total
dwarfs the price, and make each component answer a specific objection.

## Guarantees (4 types)

- **Unconditional** — full refund, no questions. Highest trust, highest risk.
- **Conditional** — refund only if the buyer completed defined steps. Filters the
  uncommitted.
- **Outcome-based** — you keep working until the result lands. Strongest for services.
- **Anti-guarantee** — all sales final. Premium positioning; only works atop strong proof.

## MAGIC naming

Name offers so the name sells: **M**ake it about them, **A**nnounce the avatar,
**G**ive them a goal, **I**ndicate a time interval, **C**omplete with a container word
("system", "sprint", "accelerator"). Example shape: "The 30-Day Local Lead System for
<niche> owners."

## Pricing psychology

- Price against value delivered, never cost to deliver.
- Anchor high before presenting the real number.
- Payment plans cut sticker shock without cutting revenue.
- Bonuses are things you could credibly charge for separately.
- **House rule:** the agent never invents or auto-fills a real price — the founder sets
  every number per deal. Use these levers to *frame* pricing, not to generate figures.

## Lead magnets

Try advertising the **core offer** directly first. When it is high-ticket or complex,
use a **lead magnet**: a free or cheap offer that fully solves one narrow problem and
reveals the bigger problem the core offer solves. Bar: good enough that you could
charge for it.

Three working types:

1. **Reveal-the-problem** — diagnosis style (site speed test, business audit). Works
   when the problem worsens with waiting.
2. **Sample / trial** — brief full access to the real thing. Works for recurring
   solutions to recurring problems.
3. **One step of a multi-step process** — give step one free, sell the rest (free
   homepage mockup → paid full build).

Delivery formats: tool/software, information, service, or physical item. Build steps:
pick the narrow problem → solve it well → pick a format → test names (small wording
changes swing engagement hard) → make it easy to consume → make it genuinely good →
end with a clear CTA plus a reason to act now (scarcity, urgency, or simply *any*
stated reason — a reason outperforms no reason).

## The Core Four — the only four ways to get leads

| Method | Audience | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Warm outreach | People who know you | 1-to-1 |
| Free content | People who know you | 1-to-many |
| Cold outreach | Strangers | 1-to-1 |
| Paid ads | Strangers | 1-to-many |

Not enough leads = not enough **skill** or not enough **volume** on these four. There is
no fifth channel hiding somewhere.

### 1. Warm outreach

Near-zero cost; almost everyone skips it. Process: build the full contact list (phone,
email, every platform) → personalize → reach out up to ~3 times → warm the conversation
(acknowledge what they said, compliment a trait, ask a question steering toward your
topic) → after a few exchanges, make an easy offer — often free first, in exchange for
a testimonial and a result. The classic move: ask if they *know anyone* who needs the
outcome; many reply that they want it themselves. Start charging once results exist.

### 2. Free content

Smallest effective unit: **hook → retain → reward**. Grab attention, hold it (lists,
steps, stories), then fully deliver the hook's promise. Respect a generous give:ask
ratio — build goodwill with many gives per ask. Scale by mastering one platform before
adding a second. Even when another channel gets attribution credit, content quietly
nurtures most buyers before they act.

### 3. Cold outreach

Strangers create three problems: no contact info (build targeted lists via tools,
brokers, or manual community digging), being ignored (lead with the biggest fast value
you can afford; keep scripts short), and low response rates (answer: volume, variety of
channels, and repeated passes over the same list months apart — circumstances change).
Cold outreach is a numbers game; know how many touches yield one engaged lead, then do
more. Routing: writing the actual email → **cold-email** skill.

### 4. Paid ads

Trade money for reach — fastest, riskiest. Pick a platform where your audience lives
and you can target it. Audience ladder: customer lists → lookalikes → filtered broad.
Ad structure: **call out** the right person → build interest via the Value Equation →
clear **CTA** with a reason to act now. Send traffic to a landing page with one offer
and one action. Spending phases: **track** money (instrument before spending), **lose**
money (testing budget is tuition), **print** money (scale the winner hard).

### LTGP:CAC — the math that governs everything

Track **lifetime gross profit : cost to acquire a customer**. Minimum viable 3:1;
target 5:1+. Unprofitable → improve targeting, improve the offer, or raise backend
revenue. Use this framing in proposals to show ROI on ad spend instead of promising
vanity metrics.

## Lead getters — people who run the Core Four for you

1. **Customer referrals** — cheapest, best-quality leads; driven by goodwill
   (value delivered minus price paid). Earn more referrals by: selling better-fit
   customers, under-promising and over-delivering, engineering faster wins, reducing
   effort, and always having a next offer. Then *ask*: two-sided rewards, point-of-sale
   asks, bring-a-friend promotions, standing referral programs.
2. **Employees** — trained to run your proven scripts; recruit them with the same Core
   Four.
3. **Agencies** — hire to learn a channel fast, then bring the skill in-house. Vet by
   niche track record and transparent reporting.
4. **Affiliates/partners** — independent businesses promoting you for a cut. Ladder of
   commitment: co-promote your lead magnet → let them sell the magnet and keep that
   revenue while you monetize the core offer → full reseller. Hold affiliates to the
   same LTGP:CAC bar.

## Operating doctrine

- **Open-to-goal:** the right action in the wrong amount still fails. Set a daily
  engaged-lead target and work until hit. Most plans fail on volume, not tactics.
- **One-page plan:** define the engaged-lead goal → pick ONE Core Four method → pick
  one magnet or offer → commit to a daily volume number → track cost per engaged lead
  → double down on what works.
- **Method selection:** no audience + no money → warm outreach. Time-rich, building
  long-term → content. Want targeted + scalable → cold outreach. Money-rich, want
  speed → paid ads.
- **Rough scaling arc:** first clients via warm outreach → repeatable process via cold
  outreach + content → scale via paid ads → leverage via lead getters.

## Applying it for clients

**New client, no audience:** warm outreach first, one simple lead magnet (audit,
checklist, free session), content cadence started, cold outreach once the warm list is
spent.

**Established client:** activate the referral system, fix the give:ask ratio, test paid
ads against the existing customer list, add complementary-business partnerships.

**In proposals:** frame every lead strategy around the Core Four; include lead-magnet
design as a named deliverable; justify ad spend with LTGP:CAC, never impressions.

**Discovery-call diagnostics:** "How do you get leads today?" (which Core Four) ·
"What share of new customers are referrals?" (goodwill gap) · "Do you have a lead
magnet?" (usually missing) · "What does a customer cost you to acquire?" (baseline for
the math).
