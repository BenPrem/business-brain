# Paid Ads Creative Design Playbook

Synthesized from deep research across six fronts: Meta static creative, direct-response frameworks, graphic-design craft, the home-services vertical, 2025–2026 performance practice, and platform specs. Percentages and benchmark figures below come from that research window — treat them as reported findings to re-verify, not eternal constants. Read this before designing any ad; run the §10 checklist before any ad ships.

---

## 0. The Five Core Truths (read first)

1. **Creative is the targeting now.** On Meta's current delivery systems, the algorithm reads the creative itself to decide who sees it. Creative diversity is the audience lever, not interest stacking; broad targeting plus diverse creative beats narrow targeting.
2. **The hook is ~80% of the job.** Roughly 70% of viewers never get past the first visual beat. If the hook doesn't stop the scroll, nothing downstream matters. Test hooks obsessively.
3. **Design AGAINST the feed, not for a portfolio.** The feed skews blue/white and polished. You win as the visual opposite: warm against cool, a real photo against stock, native against "designed." Industry analyses in this window found around 42% of top-spending ads deliberately lo-fi.
4. **Native for cold, polished/offer for warm.** Cold traffic: creative that looks like organic content (UGC, before/after, problem-led). Warm traffic: clean offer, proof, urgency. Applying the wrong style to the wrong temperature underperforms both ways.
5. **Specificity is the credibility engine.** "47 homes inspected this month," "40-year warranty," a real installed price, a real timeframe — specific numbers beat every generic superlative. (Only ever use numbers that are true for the client.)

## 1. Anatomy of a High-Converting Static Ad (the six silent questions)

The viewer asks these in order; each ad element answers one:

1. **Hook — "should I stop?"** The first thing the eye hits: pattern interrupt plus emotional recognition, or a bold specific claim.
2. **Problem/product — "what is this?"** Show the thing or the problem in use, not in isolation.
3. **Value — "why would I want this?"** ONE value proposition, not a feature list.
4. **Proof — "should I trust this?"** Real names, ratings, before/after, warranty.
5. **Offer — "what do I get?"** Specific: free quote, dollars off, financing, free inspection.
6. **CTA — "what do I do now?"** A specific verb. "Get a Free Quote," never "Learn More."

The image carries the hook plus at most one proof or claim; the primary text and headline carry the explanation. The image never tries to tell the whole story.

---

## 2. Message Frameworks (pick one per ad; don't blend)

- **PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solve.** The direct-response workhorse: name the pain in their words, make the status quo feel unbearable, present the product as the exit. Best for cold/problem-aware.
- **BAB — Before, After, Bridge.** Lead with aspiration; the split-screen before/after is BAB compressed into one image. Best for solution/product-aware.
- **Hook–Story–Offer** (popularized by Russell Brunson and Alex Hormozi). Hook stops the scroll, story bridges and proves, offer is the full package. A master frame for any creative.
- **Awareness stages (Eugene Schwartz) — the most important filter.** Match the message to what the prospect already knows:
  - Unaware → reveal the problem (free resource CTA)
  - Problem-aware → agitate and open the door (lead magnet)
  - Solution-aware → prove YOUR solution (benefit-specific)
  - Product-aware → differentiate and handle objections ("get a quote")
  - Most-aware → urgency/deal ("book now")

  Cold = unaware/problem-aware: lead with the PROBLEM, never the product. Warm = solution/product-aware: transformation plus proof. Hot = most-aware: offer plus urgency.

The #1 reason structurally sound ads fail is the wrong message for the awareness stage. Lead a cold ad with the business name and you've already lost.

---

## 3. The Hook (highest-leverage element)

A hook is a mechanism that forces a pattern interrupt — not a headline. A static ad has TWO hooks that must say the same thing: the **visual hook** (where the eye lands) and the **primary-text hook** (the first ~125 characters, all that shows before "See more").

Proven hook formulas from large-spend practitioners: confession · bold specific claim · relatability ("If you've ever...") · contrast/conflict ("Everyone does X; we do Y") · curiosity/open loop · question · number/stat · dramatic demonstration (show the result).

Test structure: **5 hooks x 2 angles = 10 variants** against one body. Curiosity hooks tend to win cold; conflict hooks tend to convert warm.

## 4. Static Formats That Win, by Funnel Stage

**Cold / top-of-funnel (look native):**
- **Before/after split** — the single best format for visual trades. Same angle, same distance, same light; labels in high-contrast type.
- **Problem-led / pain hook** — name the pain they're living with right now.
- **Forum/discussion-thread style** — creative styled like a community thread recommending the business; performance studies in this window reported material CTR gains and CPA drops vs polished controls, making it among the highest-CTR static formats. (Never fabricate an actual review or impersonate a real forum — style, not forgery.)
- **UGC / lo-fi** — phone-shot, real, slightly imperfect.
- **Us-vs-them comparison** — does the buyer's research for them.

**Warm / mid-funnel:**
- **Testimonial card** — real quote, real name and city (with permission), star rating, specific outcome.
- **Data/stat callout** — one specific number as the hero.
- **Benefit stack** — hero image top ~40%, 4–6 OUTCOME bullets, price anchor, CTA.
- **Feature callout** — annotated product.

**Hot / bottom-of-funnel:**
- **Offer/promo** — bold offer, deadline, financing.
- **Product hero** — clean and minimal, for people ready to buy.

Run 6–8 formats simultaneously (the algorithm rewards diversity), matched to temperature.

## 5. Home-Services / Contractor Specifics

Where most local contractors look amateur and lose money — and where an agency earns its keep.

**Formats that win for contractors:**
1. **Before/after** is the baseline — roughly 20% higher engagement than project-showcase formats in the cited research. Cold: swipe carousel (before on card 1, after on card 2). Warm: static split. The "before" should look worn but livable; the "after" aspirational.
2. **Offer/promo** — free inspection/estimate drives the highest VOLUME; dollars-off and financing draw higher INTENT.
3. **Testimonial** (video beats text for cold).
4. **Drone/jobsite footage** — the highest-leverage underused format; a 30-second aerial of a build in progress stops the scroll because almost no local contractor runs one.
5. **Problem-agitate** — "Still living with that drafty window?"

**Trust signals are conversion infrastructure** (a stranger is letting a crew onto their property):
- Google star rating plus review count in the corner of the creative — the single most effective trust overlay. Review COUNT outranks rating: 4.7 stars with 150 reviews beats 5.0 with 30.
- Warranty in the headline — for windows, roofing, and metal buildings the warranty is often the strongest differentiator.
- "Family owned / locally owned" — punches above its weight in markets under roughly 200k population.
- Licensed/bonded/insured — a threshold signal whose absence costs leads.
- Neighborhood social proof ("trusted by 400+ [city] homeowners," "23 projects within 2 miles") — only with real numbers.
- Years in business; the owner's face; a branded truck or uniform in frame.

**Offers that convert in home services:** free inspection/estimate (volume) · dollars off (intent) · financing (a large share of jobs over ~$5k are financed — but see the §9 compliance gate before writing any payment language) · seasonal hooks · limited-slots urgency with a REAL specific number ("14 slots this month," never a vague "limited time") · value-add over discount ("free gutter cleaning with every inspection").

**Lead mechanic:** native lead forms = higher volume, lower intent; landing page = lower volume, higher intent; click-to-message = qualify high-ticket; Google Local Services Ads = highest intent and close rate. Match the mechanic to the offer.

**The "ugly contractor ad" to never be:** clip-art logo, mismatched fonts, word-art shadows, text covering a third of the image, stock house photo, tiny phone number, every service listed, no offer, no proof, no CTA.

## 6. Offer, Proof, CTA, Urgency

- **The offer IS the campaign.** A strong offer with average creative beats a weak offer with great creative. Lead with price when price is the objection — a specific advertised service price answers the cost question before the visual finishes registering (a strategy used publicly by high-spending home-services brands).
- **Proof is the hardest-working element** (copywriting legend Gary Bencivenga's first principle). Design proof INTO the visual: star rows, dated before/afters, named results, warranty badge, the owner's face.
- **CTA:** one per ad; action verb plus benefit, five words or fewer, specific ("Get My Free Quote," never "Submit"). First-person phrasing ("Start my...") can outperform second-person.
- **Urgency must be real.** Specific, true capacity ("12 slots this week") converts and keeps trust; a fabricated "limited time" that never ends poisons the brand.

## 7. Design Fundamentals (the craft)

**Typography**
- Three hierarchy tiers (display headline / subhead-proof / body-CTA) with at least a 1.5x size jump between tiers; a Fibonacci scale (13/21/34) works.
- Maximum two typefaces (three is risky; four reads amateur instantly). Create variety with weight, size, and tracking, not more families.
- Mobile minimums on a 1080px canvas: headline 48px+, subhead 32–40, body 24–28; nothing critical below ~16px.
- All-caps needs +5–12% tracking; never all-caps for multi-line body (measurably slower to read).
- High-x-height sans-serifs hold at small sizes; heavy condensed display faces only at 28px+ and never for body.
- Crimes: stretched or squashed type, faux bold/italic, near-identical faces paired, display fonts in body, no clear hierarchy.

**Color**
- **60-30-10:** 60% dominant (mood/brand), 30% secondary (structure), 10% accent (CTA/offer/stat). If the "accent" covers 40% of the ad, there is no accent.
- **Contrast is non-negotiable:** WCAG 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large; aim 5:1+ for text over photos — ads get read in sunlight on cheap screens. Grayscale test: if the hierarchy disappears in grayscale, it relies on hue and will fail. (WCAG applies here because ads are screen media; for physical signage use the LRV standard in `guides/billboard-outdoor-design-rules.md`.)
- Break against the feed: deep navy, warm cream, or forest green stands out from the blue/white feed without going neon.
- Avoid flat brand color by building tints/shades plus one complementary accent.

**Layout and composition**
- **8-point grid:** every margin, pad, and gap a multiple of 8 — removes arbitrary spacing, reads polished.
- **Rule of thirds:** subject and CTA on intersection points; off-center beats dead-center.
- **Gestalt:** group related elements tightly, separate unrelated ones with space; the subject needs figure/ground contrast.
- **Negative space is a tool:** 30–40% breathing room channels the eye and reads premium. When tempted to add an element, add space.
- **Three-element rule:** headline, hero, CTA. More focal points means no navigation, which means a scroll-past.
- Z-pattern for visual-led ads; F-pattern for text-led.

**Image treatment**
- Real photography beats stock; UGC-authentic beats studio-polished for cold direct response.
- **Scrims are mandatory over photos:** a dark gradient at 20–40% (or light at 40–60%) targeted where the text sits — never raw text on a busy photo.
- The subject needs tonal contrast against its background; color-grade a campaign's photos as a set.
- Visually verify any stock photo — actually open and look at it — before use.

**CTA/badge design**
- The button is the highest-contrast element: rounded rectangle, ≥44px tap target, at most a subtle 2–4px shadow.
- Offer badges: clean geometric shape (never a jagged clip-art starburst), heavy type, accent color, ONE badge per ad.

## 8. Amateur vs Professional — the Tells

Amateur: three or more fonts; stretched type; default tracking on caps; low-contrast gray-on-white; four-plus colors with no hierarchy; everything centered; every pixel filled; default drop shadows, bevels, glows, lens flares, over-sharpening; clip-art; inconsistent margins; elements touching edges; generic stock; an oversized centered logo; text covering a quarter of the image.

Professional: two fonts with real hierarchy; 60-30-10 color; 4.5:1+ contrast; an 8px spacing system; off-center composition with one focal point; generous negative space; a scrim over photos; subtle, earned effects only; a small corner logo (roughly 8–12% of canvas); one message, one CTA.

---

## 9. Compliance Gate (home improvement and financing)

- **TILA / Regulation Z (US):** any specific payment amount, down payment, term length, number of payments, or APR in an ad is a "triggering term" requiring full disclosure (down payment, terms, APR) in the same ad. Default to general language — "financing available" — unless the full disclosure block is present and approved.
- **Lender co-brand programs:** if the client offers financing through a named lender program, that lender's marketing rules layer on top of TILA and are typically stricter — commonly prohibiting phrases like "as low as $X/mo," "no interest," "same as cash," "no credit check," and "pre-approved," and requiring the lender's written approval of any creative naming them. Obtain the program's ruleset and gate every financing ad on it; when in doubt, use generic financing language and route the creative to the lender's compliance contact.
- **FTC substantiation:** every objective claim (savings percentage, energy reduction, years in business, project counts) must be substantiated BEFORE it runs. "Results may vary" does not rescue an unsubstantiated claim.
- **Before/after images** must show real customer results under comparable conditions, typical rather than cherry-picked; keep the customer's release on file.

## 10. The 35-Point Pre-Ship Checklist (binary pass/fail, every ad)

**Hierarchy:** 1) primary message readable in under 3s without body copy. 2) ≤3 focal points. 3) ≥1.5x size jump between tiers. 4) CTA is the highest-contrast element.
**Typography:** 5) ≤2 (max 3) typefaces. 6) no stretched or faux type. 7) caps carry added tracking. 8) body line-height ≥1.5. 9) display faces only at 24px+. 10) headline reads instantly at mobile size.
**Color/contrast:** 11) all text ≥4.5:1, checked not eyeballed. 12) hierarchy survives grayscale. 13) ≤3 color roles. 14) accent ≤10% of area. 15) palette breaks against a white/gray feed.
**Layout:** 16) spacing on an 8px system. 17) nothing touching edges (5–10% inset). 18) extra space around the CTA. 19) off-center composition. 20) related grouped, unrelated spaced.
**Image:** 21) scrim or panel behind any text on photo. 22) scrim 20–40% dark / 40–60% light. 23) subject contrasts background. 24) any stock photo visually verified. 25) renders sharp at ≥1080px short edge.
**CTA/badges:** 26) CTA in a distinct color. 27) CTA specific and action-oriented. 28) badges clean geometric. 29) one primary CTA.
**Effects:** 30) no amateur effects. 31) any shadow is 2–4px at 20–30% opacity with a consistent light source.
**Platform:** 32) text and logos inside the safe zone (≥10% inset; vertical placements keep the bottom ~670px clear). 33) aspect ratio matches placement. 34) previewed on an actual phone. 35) (video) hook in the first 1–3s plus captions.

Contractor additions: star rating and review count present; warranty or trust signal present; offer specific; geo-qualifier in copy; lead mechanic matched to the offer.

## 11. Platform Specs and the Standard Deliverable Set

**Always produce per concept (the professional set):**
- `9x16 — 1080x1920` (Reels/Stories master; the large majority of Meta inventory is vertical)
- `4x5 — 1080x1350` (Meta feed preferred; ~20% more screen than 1:1)
- `1x1 — 1080x1080` (carousel, marketplace, Google square)
- `1.91x1 — 1200x628` (Google landscape, responsive display / Performance Max)

**Meta:** images ≤30MB JPG/PNG, minimum 1080px short edge (low resolution is penalized in delivery). Safe zones on 9:16: top ~270px, bottom ~670px, sides ~65px — keep all text and logos in a center ~950x950 box so feed crops never cut them. Primary text truncates around 125 characters; headlines around 27–40. Don't bake competitor trademarks into image text. High performers average roughly 19-word primary text and 5-word headlines.
**Google:** display/PMax images ≤5MB; landscape 1200x628, square 1200x1200, portrait 960x1200; upload the logo separately rather than baking it in; keep text under ~20% of the image; center the subject in the middle 80%. RSAs: 15 headlines x 30 chars, 4 descriptions x 90.
**Export:** sRGB, 72dpi, JPG 85–95% for photos / PNG for logos and transparency. Name files with a consistent scheme: `YYYY-MM-DD_platform_format_concept_hook_ratio_vNN`.

## 12. Testing and Velocity

- **Creative is the test, not the audience.** Broad targeting plus a 3-3-3 matrix (3 funnel temperatures x 3 angles x 3 formats). Allocate 5–10% of budget to testing; use even-distribution budgeting for tests; graduate winners at roughly 10–12 conversions.
- **Metrics:** hook rate (3-second views / impressions, target >30%) diagnoses the hook; hold rate diagnoses the body; CPA is the verdict.
- **Velocity:** creative half-life runs roughly 7–14 days at scale. Plan for continuous refresh, not one perfect ad. Swipe-file weekly from the public ad libraries (Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency Center) — ads running 30+ days are likely profitable and worth modeling. Model the ANGLE; never copy the execution.
