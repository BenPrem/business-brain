# Social Media Strategy Rules

Core rules for any social media strategy, content strategy, or content plan produced for a client.

## Rule 1: Research Competitors Before Writing Any Strategy

Never write a social strategy from general knowledge. Before drafting:

1. Research **3–5 competitors or comparable brands** in the client's space.
2. Scrape or review their actual social accounts (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Pinterest — whichever matter for the vertical), using a scraping tool where available.
3. Document per competitor: follower counts, posting frequency, the content types earning the most engagement, what's working, and what isn't.
4. Open every strategy document with a **Competitive Research** section — a summary table plus the lessons you're applying.
5. Include an **influencer seeding** section: 10–15 accounts in the client's niche worth seeding content or product to.

If the strategy contains no real competitor data, it isn't done.

## Rule 2: Build Content Pillars on SPCL

Every content strategy uses the SPCL levers (from Alex Hormozi's content framework — see `guides/hormozi-content-framework.md` for attribution and detail) to ensure the content builds influence across four psychological levers:

1. **Status** — transformation and before/after content; association with success. The viewer should feel that working with this brand raises their standing.
2. **Power** — content that gives the viewer real capability: tutorials, how-tos, frameworks. Typically the highest-converting content type for business audiences.
3. **Credibility** — proof content: data, case studies, showing the work, specific numbers.
4. **Likeness** — relatability: shared values, local references, behind-the-scenes, honest struggles.

Each pillar must activate at least one lever; across a week of content, all four must appear.

## Rule 3: Education-First Philosophy

For business audiences:

1. **Education over edutainment** — pure teaching outperforms teaching diluted with entertainment. Anyone repelled by "too educational" was never the customer.
2. **Long-form is the conversion engine** — shorts and reels build awareness; long-form (blogs, 5–15 minute videos, case studies) builds enough trust to buy. Keep a long-form engine running at all times.
3. **Volume negates luck** — set a sustainable cadence (minimum ~3 posts/week per platform) and never miss it. Consistency compounds.
4. **Revenue per view over views** — pair every vanity metric with a revenue metric. Never celebrate reach without asking what it earned.

## Rule 4: Assume Nothing in Every Post

Write every piece as if the reader has never heard of the brand:

1. No inside references — no "as I mentioned last week," no recurring-bit assumptions.
2. Introduce the brand every time, especially in video and long-form. Use the Proof/Promise/Plan opener (`guides/hormozi-content-framework.md`).
3. Every post works standalone: seen in isolation, it should still communicate who this is, what they do, and what to do next.
