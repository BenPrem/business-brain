## What Branding Actually Is

Hormozi's definition: branding is the **deliberate pairing** of your business with things your ideal customer already values. Every post, deliverable, and touchpoint pairs you with something — the only question is whether the pairing attracts or repels the person you want.

### Three Brand Metrics
Evaluate any campaign or content program on:

1. **Influence** — how likely is a viewer to change behavior after seeing it?
2. **Direction** — does it move them toward you or away from you?
3. **Reach** — how many of the right people see it?

Brand value is the product of all three. A small audience that buys is worth more than a large audience that scrolls past, which is why **revenue per thousand views (RPM) matters more than raw view counts.** Hormozi reports that reorienting his own content around these shifts materially raised RPMs, opt-ins, and sales — his numbers, from his own channel data.

### The Bouquet Test
He describes a brand as a bouquet of associations: each piece of content adds a flower (a positive association for your ideal customer) or a weed (a repellent one). Before publishing anything, ask: **flower or weed, for the specific person we want as a customer?**

## The Six Content Shifts (concept summary)

Hormozi frames his content turnaround as six deliberate shifts. Apply them to everything you or a client publishes:

1. **Education over edutainment.** Stop diluting teaching with entertainment. For a business audience, substance *is* the entertainment. If someone won't watch because it's "too educational," they were never a buyer. When in doubt, add substance, not flash.
2. **For them, not for us.** Content exists to solve the viewer's problem, not to showcase the business. If the viewer's takeaway is "they're impressive" rather than "I can use this," rewrite it. The customer's transformation is the story; your capability is never the story.
3. **Narrow over wide.** Speak directly to one ideal customer and let it repel everyone else. "Small business owners who need a website" is wide; "[trade] contractors in [region] losing jobs to competitors with a stronger online presence" is narrow. If it could apply to anyone, it applies to no one — name the audience inside the content.
4. **Revenue over views.** Optimize for revenue per viewer, not engagement. A few hundred views that produce a call beat ten thousand that produce nothing. Operating rule: **never report a vanity metric without its paired revenue metric.**
5. **Long-form over short-form (for business audiences).** Shorts build awareness; long-form builds enough trust to buy. Someone who watches a ten-minute process walkthrough is far more likely to call than someone who saw a thirty-second clip. Shorts are appetizers; long-form is the conversion engine — always have one running.
6. **Assume nothing.** Most viewers are seeing you for the first time. No inside references, no "as I said last week." Every piece must work standalone: introduce yourself, deliver, and give a next step, every time.

## The Intro Formula: Proof, Promise, Plan

Hormozi's opener for any long-form content, video, or presentation — a short structural mnemonic worth keeping verbatim:

1. **Proof** — one sentence of credibility. Specific results, specific experience.
2. **Promise** — what the viewer will *get* by the end. Concrete, not vague.
3. **Plan** — how the content is structured ("first X, then Y, then Z"). Removes uncertainty and holds attention.

Generic template: "We've built [systems] for [N] [audience] businesses [proof]. In the next five minutes you'll see the exact [process] we use to find where you're losing customers [promise]. I'll cover the three biggest issues and the fix for each [plan]."

## Production Doctrine

- **Pre-work beats post-production.** Most of a piece's quality is decided before recording: outline, research, rehearsal. A well-prepared phone video beats an unprepared studio shoot. Every piece gets a brief or outline before production starts.
- **Language beats razzle-dazzle.** A specific sentence ("we took a [trade] contractor from 2 calls a week to 14 in 60 days" — only if true) outperforms any motion-graphics budget. Invest in the words first; production value is a multiplier on a message, never a substitute for one.
- **Effective production beats overproduction.** Every production element must aid comprehension or retention. If it doesn't, cut it.

## SPCL: Four Levers of Influence

A mnemonic Hormozi uses for the psychological levers content can pull:

1. **Status** — the viewer feels working with you raises their standing. Transformations, case studies, association with success.
2. **Power** — the viewer gains real capability. Tutorials, frameworks, "here's exactly how" content. Typically the highest-converting type for business audiences.
3. **Credibility** — the viewer sees you know your field. Data, results, specificity, showing your work.
4. **Likeness** — the viewer feels you're one of them. Shared values, local references, relatable struggles.

Operating rule: every content pillar in a strategy should activate at least one lever, and a week of content should cover all four.

## The Volume Principle

"Volume negates luck." Hormozi's own publishing pace was extreme by design; the transferable lesson is that consistency and volume beat occasional perfection.

- A mediocre post published beats a perfect post in drafts. Ship it.
- Set a sustainable cadence and never miss it. For social retainer clients, treat roughly 3 posts per week per platform as the floor for being in the game at all.

## Applying the Framework

**Your own agency content:** run every post and case study through the six shifts; open long-form with Proof/Promise/Plan; track revenue per piece, not just views.

**Client content strategies:** make the six shifts the stated philosophy of the strategy document; design pillars with SPCL; pair every vanity KPI with a revenue KPI; position long-form as the conversion engine with shorts as distribution.

**Cold email:** Proof/Promise/Plan compresses cleanly — proof in the credibility line, promise in the hook, plan in the CTA. Keep it narrow (Shift 3): hyper-specific to the recipient's industry and pain.

**Proposals:** open with proof (results), promise (the transformation), plan (how you'll deliver). This maps naturally onto the StoryBrand arc (see `guides/storybrand-framework.md`): proof is the Guide's authority, plan is the Plan, promise is Success.
